Heap sort sorts a list in place, in O(n log n) time on every input. Its trick is to treat the list as a binary tree without building one: the children of position i are at 2i + 1 and 2i + 2.

It first arranges the items into a max-heap — every parent at least as big as its children, so the biggest item sits at the root — and then repeatedly moves that biggest item to the end of the list.

Phase 1 · build the heap. Starting from the last parent and working back to the root, sift down each parent: swap it with its larger child until it is bigger than both. This takes only O(n) in total.

Phase 2 · extract. Swap the root (the maximum) with the last item of the heap, which is now in its final place. Shrink the heap by one and sift the new root down. Repeat until the heap is empty.

The dashed wall in the list separates the shrinking heap (left) from the finished, sorted part (right).

        Key ideas

        What to notice.

        • The list is the tree. Children of position i live at 2i+1 and 2i+2; no pointers needed.
        • Max-heap: every parent ≥ its children, so the maximum is always at position 0.
        • Sift down swaps a parent with its larger child until order is restored — at most one swap per level, about log₂ n.
        • Build the heap in O(n), then extract n times at O(log n) each: O(n log n) always.
        • In place: O(1) extra memory, unlike merge sort’s O(n).
        • Not stable, and its jumps around the list make it slower than quick sort in practice — but it never has a bad worst case.
