A thrown ball moves sideways at a steady speed while gravity pulls it down. Together these make a curved path — a parabola.

How far it goes depends on the launch angle, the launch speed, the launch height and how strong gravity is.

Each launch draws its path; earlier trials stay faint behind it so you can compare. Every launch is saved in the Trials table.

Turn on air resistance to see how drag shortens the flight.

  1. Pick a planet, then set the angle, speed and height.
  2. Press Launch and watch the flight.
  3. Change one thing at a time and launch again.

      Key ideas

      What to notice.

      Two motions at once. Sideways, nothing pushes the ball, so its horizontal speed stays the same the whole flight. Up and down, gravity pulls it at g every second.

      Time decides range. The ball keeps moving sideways for as long as it is in the air. Anything that keeps it up longer — a higher launch, a weaker g — carries it further.

      45° is best only on flat ground. Launch from a height and the best angle drops below 45°, because the ball already has extra time to fall.

      Air changes everything. Drag takes speed away all the time, so paths lean over and land short, and the best angle drops again.

      Complementary angles. Without air, 30° and 60° (or any pair adding to 90°) land at the same spot from ground level — one flies high, one flies flat.
