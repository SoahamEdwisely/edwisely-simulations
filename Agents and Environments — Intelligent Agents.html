An intelligent agent is anything that perceives its environment through sensors and acts on it through actuators to reach a goal — a robot vacuum, a self-driving car, a spam filter.

Between the two sits the agent program: it turns each percept into an action. Every agent repeats the same loop — perceive, decide, act — and how cleverly it decides is what separates one kind of agent from another.

Three robot vacuums clean the same room, from the same starting tile, one action per step. Each action costs 1 point; sucking up a mess earns 10.

Simple reflex follows condition–action rules on the current percept only: dirty → suck, otherwise move at random. Model-based keeps an internal map of tiles it has visited and heads for unvisited ones. Goal-based has a ceiling camera that shows every mess, and plans a route to the nearest one.

The PEAS description (Performance, Environment, Actuators, Sensors) and the six environment dimensions are in the results tabs.

  1. The run is already playing. Watch the three rooms and the score under each.
  2. Press Pause, then Step to follow one percept → action cycle at a time in the Agent log. Switch the log between agents.
  3. Change the Room or the Battery and compare again. Untick a strategy to race just two.
  4. Open PEAS and Environment to see how the task is described.

Keys: Space play / pause · → one step · R reset · F full screen · Esc this index.

PEAS for a self-driving car: P safety, comfort, speed, obeying the law · E roads, traffic, pedestrians, weather · A steering, accelerator, brake, indicators · S cameras, LIDAR, GPS, speedometer.

Its environment is about as hard as it gets: partially observable (a lorry hides the road), stochastic (other drivers surprise you), sequential, dynamic, continuous and multi-agent. Chess sits at the easy end of most of these — fully observable, deterministic, static and discrete — though it is still sequential and multi-agent.

    Key ideas

    What to notice.

    • Every agent runs the same loop: perceive, decide, act. Only the decision differs.
    • Simple reflex agents act on the current percept alone. With no memory they revisit tiles and waste moves.
    • Model-based agents keep internal state — here, a map of visited tiles — to handle a partially observable world.
    • Goal-based agents choose actions that lead towards a goal. Seeing every mess makes this room fully observable for them, so they plan short routes.
    • The performance measure decides who wins. Change the battery and the ranking can change.
    • PEAS fixes the brief before you design the program: what counts as success, where it works, what it can do and what it can sense.
