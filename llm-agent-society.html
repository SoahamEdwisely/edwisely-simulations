Researchers now give AI agents a large language model as a brain, plus a memory of everything they experience. Put many of them in a virtual town and they chat, remember, make plans — and a whole society’s behaviour emerges, from gossip to parties.

Here 12 agents live three days in a small town while one piece of news tries to spread.

Each hour, agents follow their routines; when two share a place they may chat. Before speaking, an agent retrieves the three memories that score highest on recency + importance + relevance — the method of the Generative Agents study. If the news comes to mind, it is passed on.

A short buffer keeps only the latest 16 memories in mind. Reflection lets agents turn important news into a plan each evening. On Thursday evening every agent plans what to do: does the news come to mind? Note: in a real system an LLM writes each line and rates importance; here the words are scripted so the lab runs offline, while the memory and retrieval logic is the real mechanism.

  1. Press Play. Each hour, agents walk to their next place; then one speaker thinks (what comes to mind?) and talks — if the news came to mind, an envelope carries it to the listener.
  2. The strip at the top says who is thinking or speaking. On the right: inside the speaker’s mind, and a tree of who told whom. Tap any agent to pin its mind; tap empty space to follow the talk again.
  3. Switch memory to Buffer, then + Reflection, and compare Thursday’s outcome.
  4. Try the trivial rumour and the urgent water-main news.

Keys: Space play · → 3 hours on · R reset · F full screen · Esc this index.

With a short buffer, Isabella’s invitation is pushed out of mind by everyday trivia within hours, so few agents hear of it and almost none remember on Thursday. With scored retrieval, the important invitation keeps surfacing and most of the town knows; with reflection, agents turn it into a plan and most show up. A trivial rumour, by contrast, rarely beats everyday memories and fades.

      Key ideas

      What to notice.

      • LLM agents pair a language model with a memory stream, retrieval, reflection and planning.
      • Retrieval decides what an agent thinks of: recent, important and relevant memories win.
      • A short buffer forgets: news is pushed out by everyday trivia before it matters.
      • Importance matters: urgent news spreads widely; idle gossip fades.
      • Reflection turns facts into plans that persist — and the society’s behaviour changes.

      In industry

      Where this is used today.

      • Generative Agents (Stanford and Google, 2023) put 25 LLM agents in a small virtual town, where a party planned by one agent spread by word of mouth.
      • Social science and policy teams use LLM agent societies to explore how information, opinions and behaviour spread.
      • Games use LLM-driven characters that remember and plan; researchers caution that LLM agents can still behave unrealistically or carry biases.
