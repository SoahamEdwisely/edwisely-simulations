Most of the world’s electricity comes from heat: coal, gas, nuclear or solar heat boils water, and the steam drives a turbine. The Rankine cycle is the loop that steam follows in every one of these power plants.

Steam flows through four components in a closed loop — boiler, turbine, condenser, pump — and back again.

The 3D model shows the plant with the four streams flowing: HP steam from boiler to turbine, LP steam to the condenser, condensate to the pump and feedwater back to the boiler. The strip below it shows where every 100 kJ of heat goes.

Efficiency here is a simplified model: the Carnot limit from the hot and cold temperatures, times 0.88, kept between 27% and 47%. Real plants use steam tables.

  1. Drag the boiler pressure and watch the steam speed up and the boiler glow.
  2. Watch the efficiency and the 100 kJ strip change.
  3. Pick a component, or click it on the model, to see its temperature and pressure.
  4. Press Pin this run, change the pressure, and compare in Pinned runs.

Drag the model to turn it, scroll to zoom, or use Angled / Front / Top. Keys: P pin · 1–4 inspect · R reset · F full screen · Esc this index.

At 12 MPa this model gives a saturation temperature of 252.7 °C, so steam leaves the boiler at 278 °C (550.8 K). The condenser sits at 42 °C (315.2 K). Carnot: 1 − 315.2 ÷ 550.8 = 42.8%; this plant gets 0.88 of that, 37.7%.

Real plants push further: supercritical coal plants run above water’s critical pressure of 22 MPa — beyond this slider — because every point of efficiency saves thousands of tonnes of fuel a year.

    Key ideas

    What to notice.

    • A loop of four steps: boiler adds heat, turbine takes work out, condenser rejects heat, pump lifts the water back.
    • Higher boiler pressure means hotter steam, so a bigger gap between the hot and cold sides.
    • No engine beats Carnot: η ≤ 1 − TC ÷ TH, with temperatures in kelvin.
    • The condenser stays near 42 °C — it is set by the river or sea it rejects heat to.
    • Pumping a liquid is cheap, which is why the steam is condensed before it is pressurised again.
