Many processes want the CPU, but a core runs one at a time. The scheduler decides who runs next, and that choice changes how long everyone waits.

The usual yardstick is the average waiting time: the time processes spend ready but not running.

The first lane shows the input: when each process arrives and how long a burst it needs. Each strategy lane then draws its Gantt chart as the clock runs, with the ready queue underneath.

FCFS and SJF never interrupt a running process. SRTF interrupts for a shorter job, and Round Robin interrupts when a time slice runs out.

  1. Pick a set of processes and two or three strategies.
  2. Press Play or Step one time unit at a time.
  3. Compare the average waiting time on the right of each lane.
  4. Open the table below to check each number by hand.
  5. Try the Convoy set with FCFS, then change the Round Robin quantum.

    Key ideas

    What to notice.

    • Waiting time = turnaround − burst: time spent ready but not running.
    • FCFS is fair in order, but one long job at the front makes everyone wait — the convoy effect.
    • SJF gives the lowest average wait when every job is known; long jobs may wait a long time.
    • SRTF is SJF that interrupts: a shorter arrival takes the CPU at once.
    • Round Robin lets everyone start soon, at the cost of more switches. A huge slice behaves like FCFS.
