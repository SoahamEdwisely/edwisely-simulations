In an emergency room, patients are not seen in arrival order: the most severe is treated first. That is a priority queue — a queue where each item has a priority and the highest always comes out next.

The fast way to build one is a binary max-heap. Each patient’s severity (1–99) is their priority; the most urgent sits at the root.

A patient arrives = insert: they join at the end of the list (bottom of the tree) and sift up past anyone less urgent.

Treat next = remove max: the root patient is seen; the last patient in the list moves to the root and sifts down.

Both take O(log n). A plain list would need O(n) to find the most urgent patient each time.

      Key ideas

      What to notice.

      • A priority queue serves the highest priority first, not the earliest arrival.
      • A heap makes it fast: arrive = insert, treat = remove max, both O(log n).
      • Peeking at who is next is O(1) — they are always at the root.
      • Inside the heap the waiting room is not sorted; only parents beat their children. That is enough.
      • The same structure runs operating-system schedulers, network routers and Dijkstra’s shortest-path algorithm.
